# Phase A — Brief 2b: materials (and pace)

**Read after Brief 3 is done.** Same rules, same permission. Nothing touches `rules.gd`.

## Why

Milko's verdict on the phone: the world still reads as AI slop. The creature has a surface — matte clay with fine grain, a glossy eye — and everything else is flat-shaded boxes. That mismatch is the slop. This brief gives every world material the creature's physical language. **No image textures from generators.** Everything below is procedural in shaders, so it is seamless, cheap, consistent between web and native, and tunable from constants.

The material language, in one line: **matte stone and clay for what you stand on, hot glass for what kills you, gloss for what you collect.**

## Performance rule

Shaders only, one material per surface family, uniforms set per frame at most. Noise is 2-octave value noise computed in the fragment shader from world position (triplanar for monoliths, planar for tiles) — no texture lookups. Web frame time within 1 ms of the previous build. If a phone can't hold it, bake the noise to a single 512×512 tileable PNG generated by a script in `tools/` (not by an image model) and sample that instead.

## 1. Tiles — stone with grain and soft edges

- Face: `TILE` base with ±6 % luminance grain (world-position noise, scale ~0.6 units) so no two tiles are identical and the surface reads as stone, not paint.
- Per-tile variation: ±3 % hue/luminance from a hash of the tile index. Barely visible, kills the "repeated box" look.
- Fake ambient occlusion: darken the face 18 % within 0.12 units of each seam, with a smooth falloff. This is what makes tiles read as separate blocks that sit on something, not lines drawn on a plane.
- Seams stay dim (`TILE_SEAM`), width unchanged.
- Plates: armed = the face's grain remains and the wine tint sits *in* it; live = the grain washes out to `LETHAL_LIVE` with the bright seam. The wash-out over the last 80 ms before firing reads as heat.
- Slab sides: same stone shader, darker (`TILE_SIDE`), grain scale 1.2.

## 2. Monoliths — rough concrete

- Triplanar grain at scale 1.5 units, ±8 % luminance, plus a coarser 5-unit variation of ±4 % so big faces don't look flat.
- Faint horizontal banding (formwork lines) every 2.5 units, 2 % darker, 0.05 wide — brutalist concrete without any texture asset.
- Top faces keep the 12 % lighter tone; add a 0.3-unit darker band along the bottom of each monolith where it meets fog (fake contact shadow). Distance fade as before.

## 3. Hazards — hot glass

Walls, gates, sweepers, slammers: currently flat magenta boxes.
- Translucent body: `LETHAL_ARMED` at 55 % alpha when armed, `LETHAL_LIVE` at 70 % when live.
- Fresnel rim: edges facing away from the camera brighten toward white-magenta; strength 0.6 armed, 1.0 live. This gives a wall a bright outline that reads instantly at phone size.
- Inner glow gradient: brighter at the base (where it meets the floor) fading upward, 30 % range.
- Slow vertical drift of a 1-octave noise inside the body (heat shimmer), speed 0.15 units/s, 4 % contrast. Never on the rim.
- Live state adds a 2-frame white flash at the firing beat (already in Brief 3) and the rim pulsing with the beat (Brief 3's armed-breathing now drives rim strength).

Orbiter orbs and volley orbs: glossy, like the creature's eye — dark magenta body, a sharp specular highlight and a soft rim, lit by the same directional light as the creature. These are the only hazards that are lit; walls are unlit glass.

Pillars (safe), checkpoint marker, death line: matte stone shader in `SAFE` tint with the bright rim treatment of the slab edge. They belong to the floor family, not the hazard family.

## 4. Notes and goal — gloss

Notes: glossy amber spheres, lit, sharp highlight, a faint inner glow. Same family as the orbs, different colour, so "collect" and "kill" read as the same *material* in different colours — the palette does the work, as it always has.
Goal gate: hot glass in `GOAL`, rim strength 1.0, the same shader as hazards.

## 5. Background — fog with texture

The gradient quad gets two layers of slow noise: a large one (scale ~40 units, 6 % contrast) drifting at 0.05 units/s, and a smaller one (scale ~12, 3 %) at 0.12 units/s, both in world-ish space so they move relative to the scroll. Plus 2–3 huge, very faint (4 % above background) monolith silhouettes at 60–90 units, parented to the camera with slight parallax (they move at 20 % of the scroll). The background should feel like weather, not a colour.

## 6. Creature check

After the world has surface, put the creature back in frame at phone resolution. If it now reads *flatter* than the world, raise its material's specular slightly and add the same faint grain to its clay. The creature sets the standard; the world was brought up to it, not past it.

Milko dropped `fuffens_instrumental_vers_105.mp3` / `_110.mp3` and `fuffens_beatmap_105.json` / `_110.json` into `assets/audio/`.
- `song_tempo`: level 1 = 1.0, level 2 = 1.05, levels 3+ = 1.10. Load the matching file pair; beatmaps are pre-scaled, don't rescale.
- `player_speed`: 2.2× on levels 1–2, 2.3× from level 3.
- `WINDOW_DEPTH` from 2.5 to 2.2 bars, camera distance from 28 to 26 to match — less field visible ahead, so the same scroll reads faster and demands more.
